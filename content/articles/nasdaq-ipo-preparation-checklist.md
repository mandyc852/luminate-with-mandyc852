For many growth-stage companies, listing on the **Nasdaq Capital Market** is a key milestone that can elevate brand visibility, enhance credibility, and provide access to a broader pool of investors. As one of the most sought-after U.S. exchanges for smaller and mid-sized companies, the Nasdaq Capital Market offers an ideal platform for businesses aiming to go public. However, achieving a successful IPO on Nasdaq requires thorough preparation and compliance with specific requirements — requirements that tightened significantly in 2025–2026. Here's your ultimate checklist to determine if your business is ready for a Nasdaq Capital Market listing.

## 1. Meet Nasdaq Capital Market Requirements

Before diving into the IPO process, ensure your company meets the listing requirements of the Nasdaq Capital Market.

Companies can qualify using one of three standards: the **Equity Standard**, the **Market Value Standard**, or the **Net Income Standard**.

*Source: [Nasdaq Initial Listing Guide](https://listingcenter.nasdaq.com/assets/initialguide.pdf)*

Most companies opt for the **Net Income Standard**, which requires:

1. **Net Income (i.e., Net Profits):** At least **USD 750,000** in net income for the most recent fiscal year or in two of the last three fiscal years.
2. **Public Float:** At least **1 million publicly held shares**.
3. **Market Value of Unrestricted Publicly Held Shares (MVUPHS):** At least **USD 15 million** — tripled from the previous $5 million threshold as of January 2026 (SR-NASDAQ-2025-068). This minimum now applies across all three listing standards.
4. **Minimum Bid Price:** A minimum bid price of **USD 4 per share**.
5. **Shareholders:** At least **300 shareholders**, each holding at least 100 shares. At least 50% of round lot holders must own unrestricted securities worth at least $2,500.

**Important 2025–2026 changes:**

- **IPO Proceeds-Only Rule (April 2025):** The MVUPHS requirement must now be met **solely from IPO offering proceeds**. Pre-existing freely tradeable shares no longer count. This effectively sets a minimum viable IPO raise of approximately USD 15 million for non-profitable companies.
- **China-Based Companies (June 2026):** Companies primarily operating in China, Hong Kong, or Macau must raise at least **USD 25 million** in a firm commitment underwritten IPO and are prohibited from direct listings on the Capital Market and Global Market tiers.
- **Accelerated Delisting:** Companies with market value of listed securities below $5 million that violate any quantitative listing requirement now face immediate suspension with no compliance period. Securities trading at $0.10 or below for 10 consecutive days trigger automatic delisting.

Review your financials against these updated metrics to confirm eligibility before proceeding.

## 2. Assess Financial Health and Stability

A strong financial foundation is essential for a successful Nasdaq IPO. Key financial indicators include:

- **Consistent Revenue Growth**: Nasdaq investors look for steady, reliable growth. Ensure your company has demonstrated strong financial performance over the past few years.
- **Audited Financials**: Nasdaq requires audited financial statements prepared in accordance with U.S. GAAP (or IFRS for Foreign Private Issuers). Partner with a reputable audit firm to ensure compliance and address any discrepancies early in the process.
- **Cash Flow and Liquidity**: Robust cash flow and sufficient liquidity signal that your business can sustain operations and growth post-IPO. It's essential to review your working capital and liquidity ratios to confirm the company can meet its financial obligations.

## 3. Strengthen Corporate Governance

Compliance with Nasdaq's governance standards is critical for a successful listing. Enhance your corporate governance structure by:

- **Establish a Board of Directors and Key Management**: For a Nasdaq Capital Market IPO, the company must disclose its intended governance structure in the prospectus. This includes:
  - **Executive Directors and Key Management**: Names, titles, and qualifications of key executives (CEO, CFO, etc.)
  - **Independent Directors (INEDs)**: While full compliance is not required before IPO, the company must outline its plan to achieve compliance within one year of listing. Under the current phase-in rules (amended August 2024), at least one independent director must serve on the audit committee at listing, with full compliance (3 independent members) required within one year.
  - **Board Composition**: Overall composition including executive, non-executive, and independent directors. A majority of the board must be independent within one year of listing.

- **Note on Board Diversity Rules**: Nasdaq's board diversity disclosure rules (Rule 5605(f)) were struck down by the Fifth Circuit in December 2024 and formally repealed in February 2025. Companies are no longer required to disclose board diversity data, though voluntary disclosure remains an option.

- **Implement Compliance and Risk Management Policies**: Establish strong internal controls and risk management policies early to streamline future compliance and build investor confidence. Cybersecurity risk management is now a particular focus (see Section 9).

- **Prepare for Post-IPO Governance Requirements**:
  - U.S. domestic companies must file **quarterly reports (Form 10-Q)**, annual reports (Form 10-K), and disclose material events via **Form 8-K**. Note: the SEC proposed an optional semiannual reporting alternative (Form 10-S) in May 2026, but this is not yet effective.
  - Foreign private issuers (FPIs) file an **annual report (Form 20-F)** and may use **Form 6-K** for interim updates. **New requirement (March 2026):** FPI directors and officers must now file insider transaction reports (Forms 3, 4, and 5) under the Holding Foreign Insiders Accountable Act.

## 4. Assemble Your Nasdaq IPO Team

Navigating a Nasdaq Capital Market IPO requires a carefully selected team of experts:

- **IPO Adviser**: Lead adviser responsible for overseeing and coordinating the entire IPO process, sourcing professional teams aligned with your business goals
- **Early-Stage Due Diligence**: Legal counsel and auditors conduct preliminary due diligence to identify and address potential issues early
- **Legal Counsel**: Experienced U.S. securities law firms to handle Nasdaq-specific regulatory filings and compliance
- **Auditors**: Reputable audit firms familiar with U.S. GAAP standards and Nasdaq's financial requirements
- **Underwriters**: Experienced underwriters with a strong track record in Nasdaq listings to assist in pricing, marketing, and distributing shares

## 5. Conduct Comprehensive Due Diligence

Due diligence is vital for a successful Nasdaq IPO. Key areas of focus include:

- **Financial Review**: Accurate, U.S. GAAP-compliant financials — verify and resolve discrepancies early
- **Legal Compliance**: Address legal risks through a thorough review of contracts, intellectual property, and potential liabilities
- **Operational Assessment**: Identify and fix any internal inefficiencies, ensuring smooth operations as you prepare
- **Cybersecurity Assessment**: Under SEC rules effective since 2024, public companies must disclose cybersecurity risk management and governance practices. Conduct a cybersecurity assessment during due diligence to prepare for these obligations.

## 6. Craft a Strong Investment Story

A strong investment narrative is essential for attracting Nasdaq investors. Highlight:

- **Growth Potential**: Showcase the size of your market opportunity and outline realistic expansion plans
- **Unique Value Proposition**: Clearly communicate what sets your business apart from competitors
- **Proven Leadership**: Highlight the expertise and track record of your management team

## 7. Prepare Nasdaq IPO Documentation

Key documents include:

- **Form S-1 Registration Statement (U.S. Companies)**: Primary filing with the SEC — detailed overview of business model, financial performance, risk factors, and future plans
- **Form F-1 Registration Statement (Foreign Private Issuers)**: Similar to S-1 but tailored for foreign companies; includes audited financials under U.S. GAAP or IFRS
- **Prospectus**: Core component of both S-1 and F-1 filings, presented to potential investors
- **Corporate Restructuring Documents**: May include redomiciling, forming a holding company, or adjusting the equity structure

**New since 2025:** All issuers — not just Emerging Growth Companies — can now submit draft registration statements for **confidential SEC review** before going public. Public filing is required at least 15 days before the road show.

## 8. Create a Detailed IPO Timeline

A well-planned IPO timeline is crucial. The phases overlap to optimize efficiency:

- **Preparation (3–6 months or longer):** Financial audits, corporate restructuring, assembling your IPO team. Can be extended based on financial performance.
- **Filing (2–3 months):** Submission of regulatory documents while simultaneously planning investor roadshows
- **Marketing (1–2 months):** Investor roadshows and feedback, overlapping with final pricing discussions
- **Listing (1 month):** Finalize offering price, complete last steps with Nasdaq, officially launch IPO

## 9. Plan for Post-IPO Life

Going public is the beginning of a new phase. Be ready for:

- **Ongoing Compliance**: Maintain transparency with investors and comply with reporting requirements, including new cybersecurity incident disclosure obligations (material incidents must be reported via Form 8-K within 4 business days)
- **Investor Relations**: Establish a strong investor relations strategy to communicate effectively with shareholders
- **D&O Insurance**: Budget for directors' and officers' liability insurance — typically USD 250,000 to over $1 million annually, a significant ongoing cost
- **Business Growth**: Use raised capital strategically to drive expansion and increase shareholder value

## Final Thoughts: Is Your Business Ready for Nasdaq?

Going public on the Nasdaq Capital Market opens new growth opportunities but requires careful preparation and expert guidance — even more so given the tightened listing standards introduced in 2025–2026. The higher MVUPHS threshold, IPO proceeds-only rule, and accelerated delisting provisions mean companies must be better prepared than ever.
